# Align

A 24-week posture, posterior chain, hip and ankle program, run as an offline PWA checklist. Three sessions a week (Tue, Thu, Sat) of 15 to 20 minutes, on the same calendar as Phase: Block 1 weeks 1 to 8 (from Nov 2), Block 2 weeks 9 to 16, Block 3 weeks 17 to 24, then two maintenance sessions a week from Apr 19.

Static holds go after runs, never before. Strength and control work comes first in each session, mobility holds last.

## Blocks

| Block | Weeks | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | 2 to 8 | Scapular and core activation, 30 to 40 s static holds, reverse plank on bent knees |
| Load | 9 to 16 | Feet-elevated hollowed plank, straight-leg reverse plank, single-leg bridge, face pulls, loaded end-range holds, Nordics and heel drops on Saturday |
| Control | 17 to 24 | Single-leg reverse plank, plank shoulder taps, active straight-leg raise holds, 60 s static holds |
| Maintain | 25 on | Thu and Sat, about 13 min |

Test days follow Phase: Tue Nov 10 (baseline), Sat Dec 26, Sat Feb 20, Sat Apr 17. Tests are weight-bearing lunge, active knee extension, modified Thomas, deep squat, wall stand gap, and hollowed and reverse plank holds.

## Limits

- Most posture trials are in adolescents or patients with small samples and high heterogeneity. Transfer to a healthy adult runner is an assumption.
- Posture angles change more reliably than pain or function. Treat this as alignment and control work, not injury prevention.
- No trial of the reverse plank itself was found. It is programmed as a posterior chain and scapular retraction hold on mechanical grounds.
- Chin tucks and the wall stand are practice drills with weak direct evidence.
- Session time cannot reach the static stretching plateau for every muscle. Each target gets about half of it, which is why test days decide what to add.
